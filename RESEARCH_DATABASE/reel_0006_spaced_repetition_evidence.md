# Reel 0006 — Spaced repetition / interval scheduling

## Concept boundary

**Concept key:** `spaced_repetition_interval_scheduling`. This reel focuses on distributing review sessions across time rather than massing them together. It is distinct from Reel 0005, which explained the act of retrieving information from memory. Spaced repetition may include retrieval, but this reel’s main idea is the timing and spacing of review.

## Sources reviewed

| Source | Evidence type | What it supports | Required limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cepeda et al. (2008), *Psychological Science*, DOI 10.1111/j.1467-9280.2008.02209.x | Primary experiment | In more than 1,350 participants, the best interstudy gap varied with the delay to the final test; longer retention goals did not imply one universal review interval. | Do not turn the study into a universal schedule or promise a specific number of days for every learner. |
| Carpenter et al. (2012), *Educational Psychology Review*, DOI 10.1007/s10648-012-9203-1 | Review | Across learning research, separating study sessions often improves final-test performance compared with massing the same study time together. | Much evidence uses particular materials, learners, and tests; spacing is not a guarantee of understanding or performance. |
| Latimier, Peyre, & Ramus (2021), *Educational Psychology Review*, DOI 10.1007/s10648-020-09572-8 | Meta-analytic review | Spacing retrieval episodes produced better final retention than massing in the reviewed studies; expanding and uniform schedules were not significantly different overall. | “Expand the interval every time” is not established as universally superior; schedule effects vary with exposures and study design. |

## Allowed claims

1. **Research-backed:** Spaced practice means separating study or review sessions across time instead of doing the same work in one sitting.
2. **Research-backed with a boundary:** Research syntheses generally support better later retention for spaced review than for massed review in many studied contexts.
3. **Evidence-based nuance:** The useful interval depends on how long the learner needs to retain the material; there is no single magic timetable for everyone.
4. **Practical self-experiment:** Review a small concept today, revisit it after a gap, and adjust the next interval based on whether recall was easy or difficult. This is a study routine, not a medical treatment or guarantee.

## Prohibited claims

- “Review after exactly one, three, seven, and thirty days and it will work for everyone.”
- “Spacing permanently stores information” or any unsupported brain-consolidation claim.
- “Spaced repetition guarantees top marks,” “works for every subject,” or medical treatment claims.
- Presenting expanding intervals as proven universally better than uniform spacing.
