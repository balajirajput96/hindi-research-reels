## Evidence-backed statements

The reel defines spacing as separating study or review sessions across time instead of completing the same work in one continuous block. It states that many studies and research syntheses have found better later retention with spaced practice than with massed practice in studied contexts. It also explains that the useful gap can depend on how long the learner needs to retain the material, so no single timetable is universal.

## Limits spoken in the reel

The reel does not prescribe an exact number of days, present expanding intervals as universally superior, or promise marks or permanent memory. The interval advice is presented as a personal learning experiment: revisit a concept, check recall and feedback, then adjust the next gap based on difficulty.
