# Reel 0001 — Research Notes

## Working topic

**क्या आदत सच में 21 दिन में बनती है?**

## Claim boundaries

| Claim for the reel | Evidence status | Safe Hindi framing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A fixed 21-day rule is not supported as a universal habit-formation timetable. | Supported by the cited peer-reviewed discussion and the 2024 systematic review. | “21 दिन कोई वैज्ञानिक deadline नहीं है।” |
| Habit formation is generally discussed as increasing automaticity through repetition in a stable context. | Supported by the original real-world study and the review. | “बार-बार, एक जैसे संकेत के बाद किया गया छोटा काम धीरे-धीरे आसान और automatic लगने लगता है।” |
| Averages should not be treated as a promise for every person or behaviour. | Supported; considerable variation is reported across people and behaviours. | “समय व्यक्ति और काम के हिसाब से बदलता है।” |
| Simple, self-chosen actions tied to a stable cue are a practical behaviour-design strategy. | Supported as a behavioural recommendation; it is not a medical treatment guarantee. | “बड़ा लक्ष्य नहीं—एक छोटा काम, एक तय संकेत के बाद।” |

## Source findings

The original Lally et al. real-world study followed participants who selected a health-related behaviour and repeated it in a stable daily context. It modelled automaticity over time; the cited practice-oriented review describes an average plateau around 66 days and notes meaningful variation across behaviours. [1] [2]

A 2024 systematic review and meta-analysis included 20 studies with 2,601 participants. Reported median/mean timelines varied across studies and participants, including medians of 59–66 days, means of 106–154 days, and a broad reported individual range; the review also notes that much of the underlying evidence had a high risk of bias. The reel must therefore avoid presenting one number as a personal forecast. [3]

## Reel-specific communication rule

This reel is educational content, not medical or mental-health diagnosis. It will distinguish an evidence-supported behavioural principle from a guarantee: **make the next action smaller, connect it to a recurring cue, and repeat it consistently.**
