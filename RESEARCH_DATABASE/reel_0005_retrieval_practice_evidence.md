# Reel 0005 — Retrieval practice / active recall

## Concept boundary

**Concept key:** `retrieval_practice_active_recall`. The reel is about attempting to retrieve previously studied material from memory and then checking accuracy. It is not about habit-cue repetition, if–then implementation intentions, choice architecture, medical memory treatment, or a guarantee of improved examination marks.

## Sources reviewed

| Source | Evidence type | What it supports | Required limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Adesope, Trevisan, & Sundararajan (2017), *Review of Educational Research*, DOI 10.3102/0034654316689306 | Meta-analysis | Practice tests were, on average, more beneficial for learning than restudying and other non-testing comparison conditions. | Effects were moderated by features of tests, participants, outcomes, and study methods; do not quote a universal effect size or promise an outcome. |
| Agarwal, Nunes, & Blunt (2021), *Educational Psychology Review*, DOI 10.1007/s10648-021-09595-9 | Systematic review of applied school/classroom research | The review coded 50 experiments and found retrieval-practice benefits across several education levels, subjects, final-test delays, and test formats. | Most studies were conducted in WEIRD countries; findings do not prove equal effects in every learner or setting. |
| Pastötter & Bäuml (2014), *Frontiers in Psychology*, DOI 10.3389/fpsyg.2014.00286 | Literature review | In memory research, retrieval practice has been associated with later retention relative to restudy in many lab studies. | The article distinguishes laboratory evidence and educational applications; avoid asserting a simple brain mechanism or clinical treatment benefit. |

## Allowed claims in the reel

1. **Research-backed:** Retrieval practice means trying to bring information to mind after initial study, for example by answering a question without looking at notes first.
2. **Research-backed with a boundary:** Across research syntheses, practice testing can improve later learning compared with restudying in many studied contexts, but results vary with the learning task and how the test/feedback are used.
3. **Practical self-experiment:** Study a small section, close the material, answer two questions from memory, then check and correct. This is presented as a learning routine to try, not a treatment or guarantee.
4. **Explicit limitation:** A score or deep understanding is not guaranteed; source quality, feedback, time interval, task type, and the learner can matter.

## Prohibited claims

- “Active recall always beats all other methods.”
- “It rewires your brain” or any unsupported neurological explanation.
- “This guarantees top marks,” “works for everyone,” or any numerical improvement claim not given in the cited studies.
- Advice framed as therapy for memory disorders, ADHD, dementia, or any medical condition.
