# Reel 0002 — 21-Day Habit Myth, Cues, and Repetition

**Evidence category:** Peer-reviewed systematic review, primary real-world habit study, and longitudinal context-stability research.  
**Production boundary:** Educational behavior-science content; no medical or mental-health diagnosis, guarantee, or personal timetable.

## Verified findings for the script

Singh et al. (2024) reviewed 20 studies with 2,601 participants. Four included studies reported median or mean timelines to habit thresholds ranging from 59–66 days for medians and 106–154 days for means, with substantial individual variation; the authors also reported that most included studies had a high risk-of-bias rating. The reel must therefore not replace one rigid “21-day” rule with a rigid “66-day” rule. [1]

Stojanovic, Grund, and Fries (2022) followed new study habits in a six-week controlled study and analyzed app-based habit repetitions in a second dataset. In both datasets, context stability predicted greater automaticity and habit-repetition goal attainment. This supports a cautious cue-and-context recommendation, not a promise. [2]

## Required communication guardrails

The script may describe automaticity, cues, repetition, and variability. It must avoid claiming that a person will form a habit by a particular date, that a missed day causes failure, or that habit advice is treatment for a clinical condition. The final reel should recommend a small self-chosen action linked to an existing routine and a gentle restart after a lapse.
