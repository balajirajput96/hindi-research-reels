# Reel 0003 — Research Notes

## Working topic

**एक दिन आदत छूट जाए, तो क्या सब शून्य हो जाता है?**

## Unique angle

Reel 0001 addressed the false universal 21-day deadline. Reel 0002 explained a cue-to-action if–then plan. Reel 0003 addresses a separate misconception: **one missed repetition does not automatically erase prior progress.** The practical message is to resume the next realistic opportunity, while avoiding the opposite myth that consistency never matters.

## Claim boundaries

| Claim for the reel | Evidence classification | Safe Hindi framing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| In Lally et al.’s real-world habit-formation study, missing one opportunity did not materially affect the habit-formation process. | Scientific evidence from one study | “एक study में एक मौका छूटने से habit-formation process पर बड़ा असर नहीं दिखा।” |
| The study does not show that frequent lapses or long breaks have no effect. | Scientific limitation | “इसका मतलब यह नहीं कि बार-बार छोड़ना फर्क नहीं डालेगा।” |
| Habit automaticity develops through repeated behaviour in a stable context and varies substantially across people and behaviours. | Scientific evidence / systematic-review synthesis | “आदत का automatic होना दोहराव, context और व्यक्ति के हिसाब से बदलता है।” |
| After a lapse, resume at the next feasible cue rather than treating the process as reset. | Practical behavioural strategy inferred from the evidence boundary | “अगला छोटा मौका चुनकर वापस शुरू करें; खुद को ‘शून्य’ न मानें।” |
| The video does not treat mental-health symptoms, compulsive behaviour, addiction, or medical adherence. | Safety boundary | “यह सामान्य habit education है, इलाज या clinical advice नहीं।” |

## Source findings

Lally and colleagues’ study investigated health-related behaviours that participants repeated daily in the same context. Its abstract reports that missing one opportunity to perform the behaviour did not materially affect the habit-formation process, while repeated behaviour in a consistent context was associated with increasing automaticity. [1]

The British Psychological Society’s research digest explains the study’s reported limitation: a single missed day had little impact on later automaticity gains, but it also notes evidence that too many missed repetitions may cumulatively reduce the maximum automaticity ultimately reached. This explanatory source is used to communicate the limitation, not to substitute for the original research. [2]

A 2024 systematic review and meta-analysis of 20 studies (2,601 participants) reports substantial variation in time to habit formation and identifies frequency, timing, type of habit, individual choice, behavioural regulation and related factors as influential. The review also notes that most included studies had high risk of bias, so the reel must not overstate certainty. [3]

## Reel-specific communication rule

Do not say “missing one day never matters,” “streaks are meaningless,” or “you can always make up for it later.” Use singular, study-bounded language and pair it with the practical recommendation to resume at the next feasible cue.
