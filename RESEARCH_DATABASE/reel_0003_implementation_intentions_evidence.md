# Reel 0003 — Evidence record

## Topic

**Implementation intentions:** an if–then plan that links a specific cue to a planned response.

## Evidence classification

| Claim | Classification | Evidence boundary |
|---|---|---|
| An implementation intention specifies **when, where, or how** a goal-directed response will be made, often in an if–then format. | Established behavior-science framework | The formulation is described in the peer-reviewed review by Wieber, Thürmer, and Gollwitzer (2015). |
| If–then planning may help translate an already held intention into action by making the specified cue more noticeable and strengthening its association with the planned response. | Review-based mechanism | This describes a proposed and behaviorally tested mechanism; it is not a guarantee of automatic behavior for every person or goal. |
| In a systematic review of adult physical-activity RCTs, reinforced implementation-intention strategies showed a small pooled advantage in that specific context. | Context-specific systematic-review finding | The result concerns adults and physical-activity interventions; it must not be generalized as a universal effect for every habit, health outcome, or person. |
| A plan is more useful when the cue is concrete and the action is small and feasible. | Practical inference consistent with the framework | This reel presents an example, not clinical or treatment advice. |

## Source record

1. Wieber F, Thürmer JL, Gollwitzer PM. **Promoting the translation of intentions into action by implementation intentions: behavioral effects and physiological correlates.** *Frontiers in Human Neuroscience*. 2015;9:395. PMCID: PMC4500900. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4500900/
2. da Silva MAV, São-João TM, Brizon VC, Franco DH, Mialhe FL. **Impact of implementation intentions on physical activity practice in adults: A systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized clinical trials.** *PLOS ONE*. 2018;13(11):e0206294. PMCID: PMC6235272. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6235272/

## Reel guardrails

- Do **not** claim that an if–then plan cures anxiety, ADHD, addiction, depression, or any medical condition.
- Do **not** claim that planning guarantees behavior change.
- Explicitly state that the plan supports action; it does not replace rest, access, skills, treatment, or environmental support.
- Keep the example ordinary and low stakes: after placing a phone on charge, fill a water bottle for the next morning.
